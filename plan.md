Technical Specification and Strategic Roadmap for an Open-Source Product Matching A/B Testing Framework in GolangExecutive Synthesis of Product Matching and Experimental NecessityThe modern e-commerce landscape, exemplified by high-volume price comparison platforms like Cimri, operates on the foundational capability of accurately aggregating disparate data streams into a unified product catalog. This process, known as product matching or deduplication, requires identifying identical items across thousands of merchants who provide varying titles, descriptions, and imagery. The engineering challenge is twofold: a system must possess high precision to avoid conflating distinct products—a failure that leads to incorrect price comparisons and loss of user trust—while maintaining high recall to ensure that users find the best available prices across the entire market.Historically, the evaluation of matching algorithms occurred in isolated, offline environments. As demonstrated by the Shopee Product Matching competition on Kaggle, data scientists often optimize for the F1-score using static datasets. However, the transition from an offline metric to an online business outcome is rarely linear. A model that achieves a marginally higher F1-score on a historical test set may inadvertently introduce latency or fail on real-time edge cases. Consequently, the development of a specialized A/B testing tool is a strategic necessity for platforms aiming to maintain a competitive edge in price transparency.The framework proposed herein is a Go-based, open-source solution specifically architected for the nuances of the product matching domain. Unlike general-purpose experimentation platforms, this tool prioritizes the evaluation of deep learning pipelines, embedding-based retrieval, and high-dimensional vector similarity thresholds. By integrating into Cimri’s event-driven architecture, the tool facilitates a "Champion-Challenger" model of deployment, allowing for the rigorous comparison of matching logics in production with zero risk to the end-user experience.Theoretical Framework of Automated Product MatchingThe core of automated product matching resides in the ability to map heterogeneous product representations into a shared latent space where semantic similarity can be quantified. State-of-the-art systems leverage a combination of computer vision (e.g., EfficientNet) and natural language processing (e.g., Tf-Idf or Transformers) to create dense vector representations.The evaluation of these embeddings relies on the calculation of distance metrics, most commonly Cosine Similarity:$$\text{similarity} = \cos(\theta) = \frac{\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}}{\|\mathbf{A}\| \|\mathbf{B}\|}$$In production environments, the decision boundary—the similarity threshold above which two products are considered a match—is the most critical hyperparameter to test. Small adjustments significantly impact the balance between precision and recall.
